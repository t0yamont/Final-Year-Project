# Power, drag and braking increment — 28 October 2026

Implemented power_lap.py as a separate synthetic numerical model. API: PowerVehicle, PowerState, spatial_step(vehicle,state,distance_m,exit_cap_m_per_s,deployment_fraction,reserve_j=0), and simulate(vehicle,lengths_m,node_caps_m_per_s,initial,policy,reserve_j=0). Policy receives (cell_index,state), returning an electrical wheel-power fraction in [0,1]. Outputs include new state and explicit energy/work records. This does not replace lap.py or silently change the model used by existing controllers.

The spatial cell assumes linear squared speed with distance. Its duration is 2*distance/(entry_speed+exit_speed); drag work integrates k*v^2 exactly along this assumed profile. Propulsion work is power times duration, with electrical work limited by usable battery energy times efficiency. The implicit work-energy balance determines exit speed. A backward envelope anticipates downstream caps. When powered motion exceeds a cap, throttle is reduced if coasting can meet it; otherwise propulsion is shut off and friction braking supplies the required finite work. Required friction work above mass*braking_deceleration*distance is rejected. Thus speed adjustment has an explicit distance, duration, acceleration and energy cost; it is not a cost-free instantaneous clipping operation.

Validation: python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_power_lap.py -v; six tests passed. Independent cases check finite constant-deceleration braking time/work, second-order spatial refinement against exact drag-coasting speed v(s)=v0*exp(-k*s/m), refinement against exact lossless constant-power v(s)^3=v0^3+3*P*s/m, energy residuals below 1e-6 J in the synthetic test, battery reserve, deployment influence and rejection of infeasible braking/invalid inputs. These establish bounded software/numerical checks only.

Assumptions and limitations: synthetic values, open path, fixed line, no tyre/traction model, grade, downforce, harvesting, calibrated car or real track. Node caps are assumed inputs and must later carry development-only provenance. Simulated battery-limited power is averaged over each cell rather than full-power/event depletion. Throttle and braking are cell-wide controls; the true switching location is approximated and needs grid refinement on each scenario. At excessively coarse grid spacing, coasting can mathematically stall and is rejected with a refinement request. The backward envelope ignores drag's additional braking, conservatively constraining approach speed. Constant friction-deceleration availability is assumed, not inferred from real brakes. Terminal speed is an upper bound; periodic lap and exact terminal conditions remain unimplemented.

Next gates: scenario-specific convergence of elapsed time and braking locations, matched terminal constraints, physical parameter/split provenance and calibration, then fresh DP/RL comparisons explicitly versioned to this power/drag model. Existing force-model training results cannot be relabelled as results from this model. AI contribution: implementation, tests and this work record; no empirical performance conclusions.
